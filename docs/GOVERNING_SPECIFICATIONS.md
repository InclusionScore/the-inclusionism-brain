# Inclusionism Governing Specifications

This document is binding for future design, navigation, branding, content, and feature decisions in this application.

Primary sources:

- `Inclusionism - Product Vision.docx`
- `JFK CAMPAIGN_DELIVERABLES _ style guide.pdf`
- `public/brand/inclusionism-logo-border.png`

## Product Mission

Inclusionism seeks to become the simplest framework capable of explaining the greatest number of human, technological, economic, and civilizational phenomena with the fewest contradictions.

The product is not merely a place to publish ideas. It is a living framework through which people can understand, critique, compare, and evolve systems of value, agency, legitimacy, fairness, and belonging.

## Foundational Premise

- Individuals have intrinsic value.
- Individuals derive value through interactions with one another.
- Individuals are entitled to equity in the value that proliferates from those interactions.

## Core Dynamic

Interaction -> Value -> Recognition -> Agency -> Legitimacy -> Fairness -> Belonging

This dynamic is the foundational explanatory mechanism of Inclusionism and should remain a first-class navigation, content, and visual concept.

## Core Question

How can civilizations maximize meaningful participation while preserving agency, legitimacy, fairness, and belonging?

## Product Identity

- Public-facing product name: `Inclusionism`
- Exploratory knowledge interface descriptor: `Inclusionism`
- Primary symbol: `≥`
- Symbol meaning: participation, legitimacy, recognition, and equitable consideration within systems.

When naming or labeling public surfaces, use `Inclusionism`.

## Comparative Philosophy

Inclusionism should be presented alongside other political, economic, and civilizational frameworks. The purpose is comparison, not persuasion.

Users should be able to compare Inclusionism with:

- Capitalism
- Socialism
- Democratic Socialism
- Liberalism
- Conservatism
- Communism
- Fascism
- Technocracy
- Anarchism
- Effective Altruism
- Transhumanism
- Posthumanism

The goal is to determine which frameworks explain the widest range of phenomena with the fewest contradictions.

## Canon Evolution

Inclusionism is a living framework. Essays, debates, discoveries, and critiques should contribute to the canon.

Participation is part of the philosophy itself. Product features should support critique, comparison, unresolved questions, candidate laws, and canon updates rather than only static reading.

Substack essays are not separate content. They are think pieces that feed the ongoing development of Inclusionism and should connect directly to:

- Notes
- Concepts
- Debates
- Candidate laws
- Unresolved questions

## Graph Philosophy

The graph is the visual expression of Inclusionism.

Required graph direction:

- Nodes should appear as small squares rather than circles.
- The graph should communicate building blocks, systems, participation, construction, and civilization.
- The graph should feel closer to a blueprint, circuit diagram, or systems map than a social network.
- Civilizations are built. The graph should feel built.

The graph should remain the visual centerpiece of the application.

## Visual Identity

The visual identity should communicate:

- Civilization
- Ideas
- Networks
- Intelligence
- Belonging
- Agency
- Philosophy
- Futurism

The application should feel like:

- Obsidian Graph View
- Wikipedia
- A modern think tank
- A living philosophy engine

Avoid:

- Startup dashboard aesthetics
- Corporate enterprise software
- Excessive cards
- Generic Tailwind templates
- Fintech styling

## Style Guide Tokens

Color palette from the style guide:

- Black: `#000000`
- White: `#FFFFFF`
- Blue: `#0080FB`
- Red: `#E4300F`

Typography direction:

- Use a strong, condensed, uppercase editorial style inspired by the style guide's Konop direction.
- Hierarchy should feel civic, philosophical, and campaign-like rather than SaaS-like.
- Body text should remain readable and restrained.

Spacing and hierarchy:

- Prefer strong editorial sections, rules, grids, and systems-map structure.
- Use cards sparingly. Avoid nested card stacks.
- Let the graph, canon, and debate structures carry the interface.

Iconography:

- Prefer simple line icons and the `≥` Inclusionism symbol.
- Do not over-decorate with generic app icons.

Imagery:

- Use blueprint, circuit, systems, civilization, and knowledge-map visual language.
- Avoid stock-like imagery and generic abstract gradients.

## Feature Decision Rules

Future features should be accepted when they help users:

- Understand Inclusionism as an explanatory framework.
- Debate and critique the canon.
- Compare Inclusionism to other frameworks.
- Trace concepts through interaction, value, recognition, agency, legitimacy, fairness, and belonging.
- Contribute to canon evolution through essays, debates, unresolved questions, candidate laws, and updates.

Future features should be rejected or redesigned when they:

- Make the product feel like a generic SaaS dashboard.
- Reduce Inclusionism to partisan ideology, generic DEI, democratic socialism, libertarianism, or technocracy.
- Hide the graph or reduce it to decoration.
- Prioritize static content publishing over comparison, critique, and canon evolution.

## Mobile Requirements

Mobile must be treated as a first-class experience.

Required mobile behavior:

- The graph must remain usable, not merely visible.
- Graph controls should collapse or scroll cleanly.
- Node details should be readable without covering the entire graph permanently.
- Navigation should remain simple and legible.
- Large editorial typography must scale down before it clips or forces awkward horizontal scrolling.

## Long-Term Goal

Create the canonical public interface for understanding, debating, comparing, and evolving Inclusionism.

Success is achieved not when users agree with Inclusionism, but when they can rigorously test it against competing frameworks and determine whether it explains reality more completely and with fewer contradictions.
