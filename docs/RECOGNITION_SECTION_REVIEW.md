# Recognition Section Review

Date: 2026-08-03

## Canon Decision

`Institutional Sight` should not become a separate canonical node.

It is best represented as an explanatory mechanism inside `Recognition`: the institutional capacity to identify participants, understand relationships, observe contribution, receive feedback, and see how value, risk, and consequence move through a system.

## Changes Made

- Expanded `Recognition.md` with the supporting essay `Recognition Requires Seeing People`.
- Clarified that recognition is not demographic acknowledgment alone.
- Clarified that identification is not discrimination when used to understand participation, contribution, consequence, and agency.
- Clarified that identity provides context without determining moral worth.
- Connected recognition to InclusionScore's Service Catalog and Feedback Mechanisms methodology.
- Added targeted backlinks from `Participation`, `Relational Attribution`, `Legitimacy`, `Belonging`, `Inclusion Metrics`, and `Feedback Loops`.
- Updated the duplicate folder stub `05 Human Systems/Recognition.md` to point readers toward the fuller canon entry.

## Consistency Check

### Participation

The addition strengthens `Participation` by explaining that institutions cannot govern participation they cannot see. Participation remains the activity; Recognition becomes the discipline of making that participation institutionally legible.

### Relational Attribution

The addition clarifies sequence. Recognition comes before Relational Attribution because a system must first identify who participated before it can attribute value, responsibility, risk, or benefit.

### Agency

The addition preserves Agency as the return path of recognized value. Recognition does not grant moral worth; it gives institutions enough sight to return value as ownership, participation, influence, and self-determination.

### Legitimacy

The addition strengthens Legitimacy by grounding trust in accurate recognition. Misrecognition becomes a source of institutional instability because participants lose faith in systems that cannot see reality.

### Belonging

The addition keeps Belonging as an outcome rather than a slogan. Belonging emerges when people are recognized as meaningful participants, not merely counted as demographic presences.

## Additional Edits Recommended
