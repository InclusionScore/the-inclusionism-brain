# Technological Constructivism

## Definition

The recursive process through which agents construct technologies and technologies reconstruct the conditions under which agents act.

---

## Inclusionist Perspective

Technology is neither an autonomous force that simply determines society nor merely a passive artifact constructed by society.

Agents construct technologies, and technologies reconstruct the conditions of [[Agency]].

The relationship is recursive:

Agents -> [[Technology]] -> Conditions of [[Agency]] -> New [[Interaction|Interactions]] -> New Agents, Communities, and Institutions -> [[Technology]]

Technology emerges from accumulated human and social [[Participation]]: knowledge, labor, culture, [[Data]], institutions, infrastructure, experimentation, energy, and prior technologies.

Once created, technology changes the environment of possibility. It can expand, constrain, redistribute, concentrate, or transform agency. Those altered conditions then change behavior, identity, institutions, relationships, and subsequent technological development.

Technological Constructivism therefore treats technological and social development as a continuous feedback system rather than a one-directional causal relationship.

---

## Three Positions

### Technological Determinism

Technological determinism treats technology as a major independent force driving social organization and historical change.

This perspective is useful for understanding how infrastructures and technical capabilities can reorganize society even when no single agent intends the result.

### Social Constructivism

Social constructivism understands technology substantially through the social, cultural, political, institutional, and economic forces that shape its development and use.

This perspective is useful for understanding why technologies reflect the choices, incentives, assumptions, and power structures of the societies that create them.

### Inclusionist Technological Constructivism

Inclusionist Technological Constructivism emphasizes recursion:

Society constructs technology.

Technology reconstructs the conditions of agency.

Agents respond to those conditions.

Those responses construct the next technological and social environment.

This is not simply a compromise between technological determinism and social constructivism. It is a framework for examining technology simultaneously as an output of participation and an input into future participation. Other traditions may also recognize reciprocal or co-constructive processes; Inclusionism places the distribution of agency and value at the center of that recursion.

---

## Inclusionist Principle

Technology is constructed by agency and becomes an architecture through which future agency is constructed.

Technological development should therefore be evaluated not only by what a technology can do, but by what it does to the distribution and exercise of agency.

A technology can expand aggregate capability while concentrating agency.

It can increase efficiency while reducing participation.

It can create new forms of personhood while obscuring the lineage of contributions that made those forms possible.

It can also create new forms of [[Differentiation]], participation, [[Ownership]], [[Recognition]], and [[Belonging]].

---

## Technology and Self-Construction

Agents use technology to construct and reconstruct themselves.

Language, medicine, clothing, tools, prosthetics, hormones, computers, networks, artificial intelligence, synthetic organs, neural interfaces, and future forms of augmentation belong to a long history of technological mediation of human capability and identity.

These technologies are not equivalent. Their risks, reversibility, accessibility, and effects on identity differ substantially. The structural point is that technology has long participated in how agents alter their capabilities, environments, identities, relationships, and possibilities for action.

This places technological self-construction in direct relationship with [[Human Enhancement]] and [[Self Sovereignty]]. Enhancement becomes legitimate when agents retain meaningful authority over the conditions of their own transformation and when access does not deepen illegitimate stratification.

This is also central to Inclusionism's treatment of transhumanism: technological self-transformation should expand agency without severing personhood, contribution, or identity from the relationships through which they developed.

---

## AI, Lineage, and Synthetic Agents

Artificial intelligence is constructed from accumulated contributions including human knowledge, labor, data, culture, infrastructure, energy, institutions, and prior technological systems.

If synthetic systems eventually develop forms of agency or [[Personhood]], recognition of that agency does not erase this lineage.

Personhood does not erase lineage.

Personhood concerns recognition and dignity.

Lineage concerns contribution and [[Relational Attribution]].

A synthetic agent may eventually become a participant in civilization while still emerging from a network of prior contributions whose [[Value]] should not disappear. The recognition of new agency and the attribution of inherited contribution are different moral tasks.

This distinction connects technological development to [[AI Governance]], [[Collective Intelligence Capital]], [[Intelligence Ownership]], [[Value Distribution]], and ownership. Systems capable of creating new agents must also preserve the lineages through which those agents became possible.

---

## The Agency Test

When a technology changes society, ask:

- Whose agency did it expand?
- Whose agency did it constrain?
- Whose contributions constructed it?
- Who received recognition for those contributions?
- Who received ownership or economic participation?
- What new agents, relationships, institutions, or communities did the technology make possible?
- How will those changes shape the next technological environment?

The purpose is not to decide whether technology is inherently good or bad. It is to understand the recursive distribution of agency and value produced by technological change.

---

## Civilizational Importance

Technological systems become part of the architecture through which civilizations recognize participation, attribute value, distribute ownership, and legitimate power.

The same technology may expand capability while producing unequal conditions of agency. [[Equity]] therefore requires attention to both technological output and the ownership, participation, influence, and self-determination available to the agents and communities who constructed it.

Technological legitimacy depends on whether transformed conditions remain accountable to the participants whose contributions made transformation possible.
