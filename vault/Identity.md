# Identity

## Definition

The persistent pattern through which an agent understands itself and is understood by others across time.

---

## Inclusionist Perspective

Identity is neither purely individual nor purely social.

Identity emerges through the ongoing interaction between self-perception and external recognition.

---

## Core Principle

Identity is continuously negotiated rather than permanently fixed.

---

## Inclusionist Perspective

As individuals participate in communities, communities influence identity.

As individuals differentiate themselves, they influence communities.

Identity therefore exists within a dynamic relationship between agency and belonging.

## Relational Identity

Identity is experienced internally and represented relationally by others.

When 100 people observe one person, there may be 101 identities in the relational sense: the person's self-understanding and 100 representations formed through observation and interaction. These are not 101 biological persons, equally authoritative factual accounts, or fixed essences.

Relational representations can shape trust, access, opportunity, exclusion, recognition, and agency. They do not give observers ownership of the person's identity. [[Contextual Legitimacy and Relational Interpretation]] examines these representations while preserving self-definition and [[Self Sovereignty]].
