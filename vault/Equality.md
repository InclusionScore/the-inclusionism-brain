
# Equality

## Definition
The condition in which individuals or groups possess similar status, treatment, rights, resources, opportunity, or participation within systems.

---

## Inclusionist Perspective
Equality can increase fairness and reduce exclusion, but absolute equality may suppress adaptive diversity, pluralism, and legitimate differentiation.

These two are too often confused:
Equality = universal legitimacy principle
[[Equity]] = adaptive implementation principle
## Inclusionist Clarification

Inclusionism supports equality in the sense of equal legitimacy, equal dignity, equal protection under shared systems, and equal access to foundational civilizational structures such as justice, participation, and rights.

However, civilizations often begin from structurally unequal historical and material conditions.

Because of this, equitable systems may be necessary to create meaningful access to otherwise universal principles.

---

## Core Principle
Healthy civilizations balance equality with:
- agency
- participation
- pluralism
- incentive structures
- adaptive variation
- legitimacy

---

## Inclusionist Perspective
Equality is most valuable when protecting baseline dignity, rights, participation capacity, and access to civilizational systems.
